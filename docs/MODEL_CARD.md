# Model card — chest-radiograph multi-label classifier (research artefact)

## Do not deploy this

This model was trained on **synthetic images generated by `src/synth.py`**. It
has never seen a radiograph. It cannot detect, diagnose, triage, or prioritise
anything, and no number in this repository is evidence about any clinical
question. It exists to give the shortcut audit in
[`SHORTCUT_AUDIT.md`](SHORTCUT_AUDIT.md) something real to audit.

## Intended use

Demonstration and methods development: showing that a shortcut-learning audit
can recover planted confounds, and that some standard evaluation practices
cannot.

## Out of scope

Any clinical use. Any use on real images. Any comparison with published
baselines — the labels, the anatomy, the noise model, and the confounds are all
synthetic, so the numbers are not commensurable with CheXNet-era results and
should never be placed in the same table.

## Model

| | |
|---|---|
| Architecture | 3-block CNN (16/32/64 channels), global average pool, linear head |
| Input | 1×64×64 grayscale, values in [0,1] |
| Output | 3 independent sigmoid heads (opacity, cardiomegaly, effusion) |
| Loss | `BCEWithLogitsLoss` with per-class positive weighting for imbalance |
| Selection | best validation loss across 22 epochs |
| Split | **patient-level**, enforced by assertion and by test |

DenseNet-121 transfer learning is the literature standard and the right answer
with real data. It is not used here: 64×64 synthetic images do not need it, and
the project's subject is the audit, not the backbone.

## Data

2,299 studies from 900 synthetic patients, 1–4 studies per patient sharing
anatomy and pathology propensity. Prevalences 25–30%.

**Labels carry 13% simulated report-mining error.** The image renders the
patient's true state; the shipped label is a corrupted view of it. This mirrors
the property of NLP-mined imaging labels that bounds what any model trained on
them can score, and it is the reason the AUROC table has two columns.

**Three confounds were planted deliberately**, each mirroring a documented
real-world failure: a laterality marker correlated with opacity, a portable-view
border correlated with effusion, and a support-device line correlated with
effusion. Confounds are drawn from the *true* state, because in reality the
acquisition circumstance is caused by the illness, not by what the report said.

## Performance (patient-level split, held-out test)

| pathology | AUROC vs shipped labels | AUROC vs true state | sens@90% spec | spec@90% sens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| opacity | 0.742 | 0.994 | 66% | 26% |
| cardiomegaly | 0.788 | 1.000 | 68% | 33% |
| effusion | 0.819 | 1.000 | 71% | 38% |

Two operating points are reported because radiology models are deployed at
thresholds, not at AUCs, and the threshold follows the job: a triage tool that
reorders a worklist wants sensitivity, a confirmation aid wants specificity.
Who signs off on the threshold is a clinical governance question, not a
modelling one.

Run-to-run variance is roughly ±0.015 AUROC. Numbers here are single-run.

## Known shortcut reliance — the model does cheat

Measured, not assumed:

- Grad-CAM puts **1.8×** more activation on the laterality-marker box for
  opacity than area alone would predict; the other two pathologies put *less*
  than chance there.
- Adding a marker to otherwise pixel-identical, pathology-free images raises
  mean P(opacity) from **0.404 to 0.539**.
- Occluding the marker at test time costs only **−0.015 AUROC**, because the
  confound is equally present in the test distribution. **A held-out AUROC
  cannot detect this failure.**

Occlusion augmentation reduces the counterfactual effect (+0.135 → +0.097)
without eliminating it. It is a partial mitigation and is reported as one.

## Dataset-shift warnings

This model would be expected to fail on: a site with a different laterality
marker convention or position; a site whose portable-imaging policy differs; any
real radiograph whatsoever. The audit above is the reason to expect the first
two specifically, rather than a general disclaimer.

## Privacy

No PHI. The DICOM pipeline in `src/dicom_io.py` demonstrates PS3.15 Annex E
de-identification on synthetic headers, including per-patient date shifting and
consistent pseudonymisation. **Burned-in pixel annotation is not handled**, and
`deidentify()` says so at the call site rather than implying complete coverage.

## Ethical considerations

The dominant risk in imaging AI is automation bias: a clinician trusting a
model that is right for the wrong reason. This model is demonstrably right for
the wrong reason for at least one pathology, and the audit that shows it uses
three methods because the one most people would reach for — held-out AUROC —
is the one that missed it.
